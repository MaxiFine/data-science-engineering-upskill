DEFINITIONS
In business and finance, “margins” refer to how much a company keeps from its sales after covering different types of costs. They are key indicators of profitability and financial health.
Here’s a clear breakdown:

1. Profit Margin (General Idea)
A margin is expressed as a percentage and shows how much profit a company makes for every dollar of revenue.
Formula (general):
Margin = (Profit / Revenue) × 100%


2. Types of Margins
a. Gross Margin
Shows how much money is left after subtracting the direct costs of producing goods (like materials and labor).
Gross Margin = (Revenue – Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue × 100%

Example:
If a company sells a product for $100 and it costs $60 to produce, the gross margin is 40%.
Gross margin tells you how efficiently a company produces or buys its products.

b. Operating Margin
Shows the profit left after paying for operating expenses like marketing, rent, salaries, and utilities—but before paying interest and taxes.
Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue × 100%

This margin reflects how well a company manages its day‑to‑day operations.

c. Net Margin (Net Profit Margin)
The bottom line.
Shows how much profit remains after all expenses including taxes, interest, and other costs.
Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue × 100%

This tells you the company’s overall profitability.

3. Why Margins Matter
Businesses care about margins because they show:

Profitability (how much money the business actually keeps)
Efficiency (how well costs are controlled)
Competitiveness (high-margin businesses can survive downturns better)
Pricing power (strong brands often have higher margins)

Investors use margins to compare companies in the same industry.
